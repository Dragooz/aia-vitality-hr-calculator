How AIA counts these points

AIA pays out on one workout per day. Two numbers decide the tier: your average heart rate for that session as a share of your age-related maximum, and how long you hold it. Distance, calories and sport type do not matter.

Your maximum is 220 minus your age. At 40 that is 180 bpm, so 60% is 108 bpm, 70% is 126 bpm and 80% is 144 bpm.

Three things that catch people out

It is the average, not the peak. A five-minute push to 160 bpm inside a 40-minute walk will not lift the session average past 70%.

One session, unbroken. A 15-minute walk in the morning and another after dinner are two 15-minute sessions. Neither reaches 30 minutes.

Only your best workout counts each day. A 150-point run and a 50-point walk on the same day is 150 points, not 200.

Steps

5,000–7,499 steps is 25 points, 7,500–12,499 is 50 points, and 12,500 or more is 100 points.

Points do not stack

150 points a day is the ceiling, and you have to earn it from a single achievement. Walking 12,500 steps and doing a 150-point workout still pays 150 for the day, not 250. Each target has to be met on its own — part-way towards two of them adds up to nothing extra.

Yearly caps

Heart rate and physical activity points cap at 15,000 per membership year. Steps cap separately at 10,000.

If your watch disagrees

Garmin, Apple and Fitbit often estimate a personal maximum from your training history, which can sit 10–20 bpm either side of 220 minus age. AIA scores against the formula, so use these targets for points and your watch's zones for training.
