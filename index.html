How to calculate AIA Vitality points

AIA gives points for one workout each day. Two values set the points: your average heart rate and the duration of the workout.

  1. Find your maximum heart rate: 220 minus your age.
  2. Work out 60%, 70% and 80% of it. Round up to the next whole bpm.
  3. Check which percentage your average heart rate reached.
  4. Look up the minutes in the table below. AIA gives the highest tier you reach.

Example: you are 30, so your maximum is 190 bpm. The targets are 114 bpm (60%), 133 bpm (70%) and 152 bpm (80%). A 35-minute run at an average of 145 bpm reaches 70% for 30 minutes or more. You earn 100 points.

Do heart rate and step points add up?

No. The daily maximum is 150 points. AIA gives the higher of your workout points and your step points. It does not add them.

Yearly limits

Heart rate and physical activity: 15,000 points each membership year. Steps: 10,000 points each membership year.

If your watch shows different zones

Your watch can calculate a different maximum heart rate. AIA uses 220 minus your age. Use the targets on this page for points.

Common questions

Why did I only get 50 points instead of 100?

Usually the session average fell below the threshold, or the session ran short. AIA uses the average across the whole workout, not your peak.

Does AIA Vitality use average or peak heart rate?

Average, across a single session. A short sprint that lifts your peak does not qualify the session if the average stays below the threshold.

Do two short walks count as one session?

No. Two 15-minute walks are two sessions of 15 minutes, so neither reaches the 30-minute threshold.

What heart rate do I need for 150 points?

30 minutes at 80% of your maximum, 60 minutes at 70%, or 90 minutes at 60%. For a 35-year-old with a 185 bpm maximum, that is 30 minutes at 148 bpm, 60 minutes at 130 bpm, or 90 minutes at 111 bpm.

How many heart rate points can I earn in a day?

Heart rate points are awarded once per day. If your tracker captures more than one workout, AIA awards the workout worth the most points, not the total.
