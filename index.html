How AIA Vitality counts heart rate points

AIA Vitality pays out on a single workout, once per day. Two numbers decide the tier: your average heart rate for that session as a share of your age-related maximum, and how long you sustain it. Nothing else feeds into it — not distance, not calories, not the sport.

Your age-related maximum is 220 minus your age. At 40 that is 180 bpm, so 60% is 108 bpm, 70% is 126 bpm, and 80% is 144 bpm. This is a population average rather than your personal ceiling, but it is the number AIA credits against, so it is the one worth training to.

The three things that catch people out

It is the average, not the peak. A five-minute push to 160 bpm inside a 40-minute walk will not carry the session average past 70%. The tier is decided by the whole workout.

One session, unbroken. A 15-minute walk in the morning and another after dinner are two 15-minute sessions. Neither reaches the 30-minute threshold, so neither pays.

Only your best workout counts each day. If your tracker captures three activities, AIA awards the one worth the most points, not the sum. A 150-point run and a 50-point walk on the same day is 150 points, not 200.

Worked example

A 35-year-old has a maximum of 185 bpm (220 − 35). To reach the top tier they need one of: 30 minutes averaging 148 bpm, 60 minutes averaging 130 bpm, or 90 minutes averaging 111 bpm. The 90-minute option is a brisk walk; the 30-minute option is a hard run. Both pay 150 points.

Yearly limits

Heart rate and physical activity points cap at 15,000 per membership year. Steps cap separately at 10,000. Hitting 150 points every day would reach the activity cap in about 100 days, so the cap binds well before the year is out for anyone training consistently.

Common questions

Why did I only get 50 points instead of 100?

Usually the session average landed under the threshold, or it ran short. Check the average your watch reported rather than the peak, then compare it against the table above. Being two minutes short of 30 minutes drops a 100-point session to 50.

What if my watch shows a different maximum heart rate?

Garmin, Apple and Fitbit often estimate a personal maximum from your training history, and it can sit 10 to 20 bpm either side of 220 minus age. AIA scores against the formula regardless, so use the targets on this page for points and your watch's zones for training.

Does walking earn heart rate points?

Yes, if the average is high enough for long enough. For most people a brisk walk sits near 60% of maximum, which pays 50 points at 30 minutes, 100 at 60 minutes and 150 at 90 minutes.

Do heart rate points and step points stack?

Yes. They are separate categories with separate caps, so a day can earn both. Only heart rate points are limited to your single best workout.

How long until points show on my statement?

That depends on your tracker syncing to AIA Vitality, not on this page. If a workout is missing, check the device connection in the AIA Vitality app first.
